\paragraph{Physical chart and exact gauge.}
Let $\tau=T-t=q(1-\eta^2)$, $z=q^D\eta$, $X=r^2/(2q)$, $A=1/2+h$, $D=1/2-h$ and
\[
\mathcal Z_a F=\frac{2a\eta F+(1-\eta^2)F_\eta-2\eta XF_X}{1-2h\eta^2}.
\]
For compact, axis-regular smooth functions $\Phi,B$ flat at their outer boundaries put
\[
A_r=0,\quad A_\theta=q^{-h}\Phi/\sqrt{2X},\quad A_z=-q^{-h}B,\quad U=\nabla\times A.
\]
Then
\[
U_r=q^{-1/2}V_r,\quad U_z=q^{-A}V_z,\quad U_\theta=q^{-A}V_\theta,\qquad
V_r=-\frac{\mathcal Z_{1-A}\Phi}{\sqrt{2X}},\;
V_z=\Phi_X,\;
V_\theta=\sqrt{2X}B_X.
\]
This is identically solenoidal and extends smoothly by zero to a periodic chart, provided $\Phi,B=O(X)$ at the axis. Conversely compact, regular leading poloidal profiles with zero slice flux obey this representation with $\Phi(X,\eta)=\int_0^X V_z(Y,\eta)dY$.
\paragraph{Axial return-flow law.}
For a compact axisymmetric solenoidal field with support in a proper axial subinterval, integrating incompressibility at fixed physical $z$ gives
\[
\boxed{\int_0^\infty U_z(t,r,z)r\,dr=0},\qquad
\boxed{\int_0^{X_*}V_z(X,\eta)\,dX=0}.
\]
Thus a positive central axial jet necessarily has a compensating negative annular flow. If $V_z\ge m$ on $0<X<x_0$, then
\[
\int_{x_0}^{X_*}V_z^2\,dX\ge\frac{m^2x_0^2}{X_*-x_0}
\]
by Cauchy--Schwarz.
\begin{theorem}[Sharper axisymmetric disk energy bound]
Let $U$ be smooth, compactly confined in disks $r\le R_*(t)$, axisymmetric, solenoidal, and supported in a proper axial subinterval. With $\theta_z=\|U_z\|_2^2/\|U\|_2^2$,
\[
\boxed{\frac{\|\nabla U\|_2^2}{\|U\|_2^2}
\ge\frac{j_{1,1}^2+(j_{2,1}^2-j_{1,1}^2)\theta_z}{R_*^2}}
\]
where $j_{1,1}^2=14.68197064\ldots$ and $j_{2,1}^2=26.37461643\ldots$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The horizontal Cartesian components of axisymmetric $U$ have angular Fourier order one, whose Dirichlet disk eigenvalue is $j_{1,1}^2/R_*^2$. The axial component is radial and has zero disk mean by the flux law. The sharp zero-mean radial Dirichlet quotient is $j_{2,1}^2/R_*^2$: its constrained Euler--Lagrange equation is $-(rf')'=\lambda rf+\mu r$, giving $f(r)\propto J_0(kr)-J_0(kR_*)$. The zero mean condition is $2J_1(kR_*)=kR_*J_0(kR_*)$, equivalent to $J_2(kR_*)=0$. Add the two disk-sector bounds at every physical $z$ and then the nonnegative axial gradient energy.
\end{proof}
