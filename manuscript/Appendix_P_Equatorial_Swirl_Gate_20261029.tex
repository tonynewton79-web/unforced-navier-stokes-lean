\begin{theorem}[Flat compact equatorial swirl defeats two-mode raw source gain]
Let $U$ be a smooth periodic axisymmetric solenoidal prepared field supported in a compact physical similarity chart $\tau=T-t=q(1-\eta^2)$, $X=r^2/(2q)$, $z=q^D\eta$, $A=1/2+h$, $D=1/2-h$, $0<h<1/6$. Assume $U_r$ is odd in physical $z$, $U_\theta,U_z$ even, and $U_\theta=q^{-A}V_\theta(X,\eta)$ with nonzero $V(X)=V_\theta(X,0)\not\equiv0\text{ is smooth for }X>0\text{ and flat at }X_*$ flat at $X_*>0$. Assume the higher-order azimuthal source remainder is $O(\tau^{-A-1+2h})$ uniformly in profile coordinates, and $\lambda(t)=\tau c_A(t)\to\lambda_*\in\mathbb R$, where $c_A=\langle G,U\rangle/\|U\|_2^2$ and $G=U_t-\Delta U+\mathbb P[(U\cdot\nabla)U]$. Let $W=\partial_zU$ and $Q_{AT}$ project orthogonally to both $U$ and $W$. Then
\[
\boxed{\|Q_{AT}G(t)\|_2\ge c\,\tau^{-3/4-3h/2}}
\]
for all sufficiently small $\tau$, with some profile-dependent $c>0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The global periodic pressure need not be axisymmetric, because the torus lacks continuous rotational symmetry. Nonetheless, on each physical circle the angular average of its azimuthal gradient is zero: $(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}r^{-1}\partial_\theta p\,d\theta=0$. Since $U,W$ are axisymmetric, the angular average of the residual equals the unprojected azimuthal defect minus both mode projections. At the equator $z=0$, $U_r=\partial_zU_\theta=W_\theta=0$ by reflection, so azimuthal advection and the translation projection vanish. The physical vector Laplacian gives
\[
\overline{(Q_{AT}G)_\theta}(t,r,0)
=\tau^{-A-1}\mathcal D_{\lambda(t)}V(X)+O(\tau^{-A-1+2h}),
\quad
\mathcal D_\lambda V=-2XV''+(X-2)V'
+\left(A-\lambda+\frac1{2X}\right)V.
\]
If $\mathcal D_{\lambda_*}V$ vanished everywhere on $(0,X_*)$, ODE uniqueness with flat outer data $V(X_*)=V'(X_*)=0$ would force $V\equiv0$, a contradiction. A nonzero point persists on an open similarity-coordinate neighborhood and the exact physical Jacobian is
$2\pi\tau^{1+D}(1-2h\eta^2)(1-\eta^2)^{-D-2}\,dX\,d\eta$.
Jensen's inequality controls the angular average by the full vector norm; taking the $L^2$ norm yields $\tau^{-A-1+(1+D)/2}=\tau^{-3/4-3h/2}$.
\end{proof}
