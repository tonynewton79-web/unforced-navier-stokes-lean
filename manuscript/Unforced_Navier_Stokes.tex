\begin{abstract}
The absence of external forcing makes the three-dimensional Navier--Stokes
regularity problem substantially more restrictive than a construction with
prescribed smooth forcing. We study exact mathematical prerequisites for a
candidate unforced finite-time singularity on the periodic three-torus.
An explicit compact symmetric-divergence inverse is proved for smooth
axisymmetric meridional sources supported in an annulus, with vanishing
total axial force as the necessary and sufficient compatibility condition.
A separate two-diagonal pressure-neutral construction exhibits a sharp
radial sign obstruction. Finite seven-ray stress algebra yields the exact
coefficient margin $15971/122900$ and sharp componentwise amplification
factor $14$. A compact source-matched dual test excludes a strong
physical entrywise stress bound at the corresponding numerical scale.
For the frozen cylindrical seven-ray frame, an exact matrix
reconstruction identifies that bound with a sufficient four-coordinate
H8 stress estimate. We give the exact three-inequality description
and sharp dual support function of the original seven-coefficient cone.
An exact rational Bernstein certificate shows that even this larger
absolute coefficient budget fails for the specified manufactured
meridional source. The larger, one-sided seven-ray positivity cone
has a distinct asymmetric sharp dual support function. An exact
rational enclosure shows that the same dual field does not obstruct
this larger cone; no inverse is thereby constructed. A kernel-checked
matrix identity then transfers the exact positive-cone dual to any
rotating source frame, yielding a necessary integrated bound without
assuming the frame is constant. None of these diagnostic tests
decides the authentic H8 residual. We also derive the exact
pressure-compensated annular flux identity and its two sharp
frame-dependent cone inequalities. The pressure boundary trace is
essential: an axial source primitive alone cannot be bounded by the
stress cone when that trace is free.
The recovered 122-page Case~006 revision also gives an exact
source-native axial interval under the original absolute coefficient
budget; its interval is not a necessary condition under one-sided
coefficient positivity alone.
The source-prescribed separable shell shaper also admits an exact
quadratic flux reduction, including its often-leading viscous shear.
An endpoint uniqueness lemma rules out an $O(b^2)$ stress-shear budget
for any nonzero compact cutoff with a nondegenerate viscous second-jet
coefficient, provided the remaining terms have at most first-jet
size. The hypothesis is not established for the complete H8 residual.
Normalized quarter-turn localization requires twelve ordered nonlinear
overlap terms; an explicit local construction shows that these terms
can carry three quarters of the core kinetic shear. A separately chosen
smooth annular cutoff and normalized toroidal pressure illustrate the
remaining source choices, and a combined rational example checks the
full flux ledger against direct stress integration. These are test
functions, not the original unfrozen H8 cutoff or pressure.
For a core localization sharing the shaper cutoff, a cylindrical
circulation identity reduces cancellation of the viscous second jet
to an exact axial-core moment and one reference-circle trace.
For independent core and shaper cutoffs we retain every second
spatial derivative, the full axial pressure jump and a rigorous
variable-frame enclosure of the H8 shear support.
The time derivative of the localized velocity remains a separate
source marginal; its radial primitive cannot be discarded.
Lean additionally checks the meridional pointwise inverse and both
integral antiderivative identities, alongside the finite cone algebra,
physical residual expansion, and conditional terminal-speed retention.
These results do not yet construct the
source-matched, scale-sharp, positive-cone-compatible compact stress or
a genuine zero-force singular solution. The global unforced existence
question remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and the mathematical objective}

We work at viscosity one on the unit three-torus
$\T=\R^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}$. For a smooth velocity $u(t,x)\in\R^{3}$
and pressure $p(t,x)\in\R$, the unforced incompressible equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:NS}
  \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\Delta u+\nabla p=0,
  \qquad \diver u=0.
\end{equation}
The specific target is to find nonzero smooth divergence-free periodic
initial data and a solution smooth for $0\le t<1$, of finite kinetic
energy on a fundamental cell, whose speed becomes unbounded as $t\uparrow1$.
The source-free condition in \eqref{eq:NS} is essential: a verified
construction with a nonzero smooth force does not establish this target.
The Clay problem statement makes this distinction explicit
\cite{Fefferman2000}. The published OpenAI Lean formalization states
Navier--Stokes breakdown with smooth forcing; its unforced finite-time
result concerns Euler rather than Navier--Stokes \cite{OpenAI2026}.

Our approach isolates small results that are meaningful independently of
the unresolved global theorem. First, a source must admit a compact
symmetric-divergence correction. Second, if that correction must remain
in a prescribed positive stress cone, its components need quantitative
control relative to a density envelope. Third, all terms in the actual
nonlinear physical residual must cancel with a single periodic pressure.
Finally, a singular leading trace can survive only if the correction is
provably subleading. Sections 2--7 establish exact parts of this chain.

The construction of compact symmetric anti-divergence operators under
force and torque compatibility is not new; see, for example, the
support-preserving operator and its explicit moment correction in
\cite[\S3.6 and Appendix A]{Zhao2026}. The direct axisymmetric formula
below is offered as an elementary source-specific coordinate realization,
not as a priority claim for compact symmetric anti-divergence. The new
obstacle for the present programme is the additional shrinking-support,
weighted-seven-ray positivity requirement, which is not supplied by
general existence of a compact tensor. Section~\ref{sec:dual} also
shows that a natural \emph{stronger} physical-component envelope is
incompatible with one explicit source, even modulo pressure gradients.
We subsequently exclude the original absolute coefficient envelope
for the same manufactured source by a sharper dual calculation;
we then determine the sharp support of the distinct one-sided cone
and test the same source against it. The exact frame pullback supplies
the correct dual coordinates for the genuine spatially varying source.
The actual H8 source remains a separate problem.

\section{A compact symmetric inverse in an annular region}

Let $0<a<b$ and $c<d$, and write
$\Omega=(a,b)\times(c,d)$ in meridional cylindrical coordinates $(r,z)$.
All functions in this section are independent of the azimuthal angle
$\theta$. The orthonormal cylindrical frame is $(e_r,e_\theta,e_z)$.
For a symmetric tensor with components
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stress}
  S=K\,e_r\otimes e_r+L\,e_\theta\otimes e_\theta
    +M(e_r\otimes e_z+e_z\otimes e_r)
    +C\,e_z\otimes e_z,
\end{equation}
standard cylindrical differentiation gives
\begin{align}
 (\diver S)_r&=\partial_rK+\frac{K-L}{r}+\partial_zM,
       \label{eq:divr}\\
 (\diver S)_z&=\partial_rM+\frac Mr+\partial_zC,
       \label{eq:divz}\\
 (\diver S)_\theta&=0.\notag
\end{align}
The $1/r$ terms are geometric, not lower-order terms that may be
discarded near a shrinking cylindrical support.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit compact meridional inversion]\label{thm:inverse}
Let $f_r,f_z\in\cc(\Omega)$, and extend
$f=f_r e_r+f_z e_z$ by zero to $\R^{3}$ in cylindrical coordinates.
There is a symmetric $S\in\cc(\R^{3};\operatorname{Sym}_3)$,
axisymmetric and supported in the same open annular cylinder, with
$\diver S=f$, if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vertical-force}
 \int_a^b\int_c^d r f_z(r,z)\dd z\dd r=0.
\end{equation}
The construction uses no nonlocal spatial projection, but it asserts no
positive-semidefinite or weighted-cone bound on $S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For necessity, integrate the axial component of $\diver S=f$
against the cylindrical Jacobian $r$. Compact support and
\eqref{eq:divz} give
\[
 \int_a^b\int_c^d r f_z\dd z\dd r
 =\int_a^b\int_c^d\bigl[\partial_r(rM)+r\partial_zC\bigr]
   \dd z\dd r=0.
\]
For sufficiency, define the axial source marginal and its radial
primitive by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HJ}
 J(r)=\int_c^d f_z(r,z)\dd z,
 \qquad H(r)=\frac1r\int_a^r sJ(s)\dd s.
\end{equation}
Since $r>0$ on $[a,b]$, $H$ is smooth. The vanishing moment
\eqref{eq:vertical-force} makes $H$ identically zero outside a compact
subinterval of $(a,b)$. Choose any $\eta\in\cc((c,d))$ normalized by
$\int_c^d\eta(z)\dd z=1$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M}
 M(r,z)=H(r)\eta(z).
\end{equation}
The identity $(rH)'=rJ$ implies
\[
 \int_c^d\left(f_z-\partial_rM-\frac Mr\right)\dd z
   =J-H'-\frac Hr=0.
\]
Consequently the axial primitive
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C}
 C(r,z)=\int_c^z\left[f_z(r,\zeta)-\partial_rM(r,\zeta)
                  -\frac{M(r,\zeta)}r\right]\dd\zeta
\end{equation}
vanishes both below and above the axial support; it is smooth and
compactly supported in $\Omega$. Choose any $K\in\cc(\Omega)$,
including $K=0$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 L=K+r\bigl(\partial_rK+\partial_zM-f_r\bigr).
\end{equation}
Each component of \eqref{eq:stress} is smooth and compactly supported
away from the axis. Substitution in \eqref{eq:divr} gives
\[
 \partial_rK+\frac{K-L}{r}+\partial_zM
 =\partial_rK-(\partial_rK+\partial_zM-f_r)+\partial_zM=f_r,
\]
and \eqref{eq:C} gives $\partial_rM+M/r+\partial_zC=f_z$.
Thus $\diver S=f$ as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the construction]
The theorem concerns a meridional source with $f_\theta=0$ and support
strictly separated from $r=0$. An azimuthal source introduces a
different angular-momentum compatibility condition, and extending
the construction across the axis requires separate regularity
conditions on the cylindrical components. The tensor in the theorem
may be indefinite, and its size can deteriorate when the support
shrinks. None of these omissions is harmless for the weighted H8
attachment problem.
\end{remark}

\section{Pressure-neutral gauges and a sign obstruction}

The mixed and hoop degrees of freedom in Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse} are
not optional decorations. A restricted diagonal ansatz exhibits a
genuine obstruction to maintaining one sign.

\begin{proposition}[Two-diagonal gauge]\label{prop:gauge}
Let $F\in\cc(\Omega)$, put $K=rF_r$ and $C=F+rF_r$, and take
$S=K e_r\otimes e_r+C e_z\otimes e_z$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge}
 \diver S=\nabla C.
\end{equation}
In particular its divergence is a pressure gradient, so it cannot by
itself represent a prescribed source with a nonzero solenoidal part.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The radial divergence is $K_r+K/r$. Since $K=rF_r$,
$C_r=F_r+K_r=K/r+K_r$. The axial divergence is $C_z$,
which proves \eqref{eq:gauge}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Restricted radial sign condition]\label{prop:sign}
If $K$ and $C$ are compactly supported in $r\in(a,b)$ at a fixed $z$
and obey $C_r=K_r+K/r$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:momentK}
 \int_a^b K(r,z)/r\dd r=0.
\end{equation}
Consequently, $K\ge0$ forces $K\equiv0$ for this restricted two-diagonal
class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Integrate the derivative identity between $a$ and $b$;
the endpoint terms of $K$ and $C$ vanish by compact support.
Since $r>0$, continuity and \eqref{eq:momentK} imply that a
nonnegative $K$ is identically zero.
\end{proof}

This conclusion does not exclude a sign-changing correction around a
positive background tensor or a compact construction with nonzero
hoop and mixed components. Indeed, Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse} explicitly
uses both of those components. The more difficult question is whether
one can choose them while retaining a prescribed strict coefficient
cone with uniform shrinking-support estimates.

\section{The exact seven-ray coefficient margin}

The finite cone appearing in the current H8 source has seven rays.
With aperture $t=1/4$ they are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rays}
\begin{aligned}
 v_1&=(1,0,0), &v_2&=(1,t,0),&v_3&=(1,-t,0),\\
 v_4&=(1,0,t),&v_5&=(1,0,-t),\\
 v_6&=(1,t,t),&v_7&=(1,t,-t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Their normalized positive coefficient centre is
$c^{(0)}_i=236/1229$ for $i=3$ and $172/1229$ for every other
index. The raw source covariance and its trace-free rank-one identity
are established by exact finite arithmetic and in
\texttt{UnforcedPatching.SevenRayCenter.lean}.

Write $(A,B,C,D)\in\R^4$ for the four trace-free stress coordinates.
The resulting coefficient perturbations, in ray order, are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-map}
 \delta c=\left(
 -\tfrac32 D,\ 2C-4D,\ -2C-4D,\
 2A-8B+4D,\ -2A+8B+4D,\ 8B,\ -8B\right).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Sharp componentwise cone estimate]\label{prop:14}
For $m=\max(|A|,|B|,|C|,|D|)$ the map
\eqref{eq:linear-map} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:14}
 \max_{1\le i\le7}|\delta c_i|\le14m,
\end{equation}
and the number $14$ is optimal. If $\rho\ge0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps}
 m\le\frac{15971}{1720600}\rho,
\end{equation}
then for every ray $c_i^{(0)}\rho+\delta c_i\ge\rho/100$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The absolute row sums in \eqref{eq:linear-map} are respectively
$3/2$, $6$, $6$, $14$, $14$, $8$, $8$; hence the operator norm
from $\ell^\infty(\R^4)$ to $\ell^\infty(\R^7)$ is at most $14$.
At $(A,B,C,D)=(1,-1,0,1)$, the fourth coordinate equals $14$,
so the constant is sharp. Since
\[
 \frac{172}{1229}-\frac1{100}=\frac{15971}{122900},
\]
inequality \eqref{eq:eps} implies
$|\delta c_i|\le 14m\le(15971/122900)\rho$.
As $c_i^{(0)}\ge172/1229$, the claimed floor follows.
\end{proof}

The seven-ray claim is a finite linear-algebra statement. It provides
the exact smallness target for a potential compact correction but gives
no estimate for the particular tensor delivered by
Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse}. In particular, no implication from
unweighted compact solvability to \eqref{eq:eps} is available.

\begin{proposition}[The frozen-frame physical tensor map]\label{prop:frame}
In the local co-rotating frame $(e_1,e_2,e_3)=(e_r,e_\theta,e_z)$
of the seven-ray construction, let
$G=\sum_{i=1}^7\delta c_i v_i\otimes v_i$.
The exact physical tensor perturbation and its trace-free pressure
representative are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:four-field-map}
 G=\begin{pmatrix}
 -\frac32D&C&A\\ C&-\frac12D&B\\ A&B&\frac12D
 \end{pmatrix},\quad
 H=G+\frac D2 I
 =\begin{pmatrix}-D&C&A\\ C&0&B\\ A&B&D\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
In particular, if $m=\max(|A|,|B|,|C|,|D|)$, then
$\max_{i,j}|H_{ij}|=m$ in that frame.
Conversely, the coordinates are recovered as
$A=H_{rz}$, $B=H_{\theta z}$, $C=H_{r\theta}$, and $D=H_{zz}$.
The image is the four-dimensional subspace of symmetric trace-free
matrices with $H_{\theta\theta}=0$ (hence $H_{rr}=-H_{zz}$);
one independent trace-free diagonal mode is absent.
For a different orthonormal frame with columns $E$, the physical matrix
is $EHE^{\mathsf T}$; its componentwise maximum need not equal $m$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute the seven vectors \eqref{eq:rays} and their coefficient
perturbations \eqref{eq:linear-map} into the six independent entries
of $G$. The diagonal entries are $-3D/2,-D/2,D/2$; the off-diagonal
entries in $(r\theta,rz,\theta z)$ order are $(C,A,B)$.
Thus $\operatorname{tr}G=-3D/2$, and the scalar gauge addition
$D I/2$ removes its trace. The maximum-entry identity then follows
directly from the displayed matrix. Conjugation gives the last claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The exact original seven-coefficient cone]
\label{prop:exact-cone}
For any $\tau\ge0$, all seven constraints $|\delta c_i|\le\tau$
of \eqref{eq:linear-map} hold if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-cone}
 4|D|+2|C|\le\tau,\qquad
 4|D|+2|A-4B|\le\tau,\qquad
 8|B|\le\tau.
\end{equation}
The sufficient bound $m\le\tau/14$ therefore need not hold for a
valid coefficient perturbation: at
$(A,B,C,D)=(\tau,\tau/8,0,0)$, the exact largest coefficient
perturbation and $m$ both equal $\tau$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For real $x,y$ the identity
$\max(|x+y|,|x-y|)=|x|+|y|$ is exact.
Apply it to the paired coefficients $(\delta c_2,\delta c_3)$,
$(\delta c_4,\delta c_5)$, and $(\delta c_6,\delta c_7)$.
The first pair contributes $4|D|+2|C|$, the second
$4|D|+2|A-4B|$, and the third $8|B|$. The remaining
coefficient $|\delta c_1|=3|D|/2$ is dominated by the first
pair. Substitution verifies the equality case.
\end{proof}

This identifies a previously implicit assumption: the bound
\eqref{eq:eps} is also a physical entrywise bound for the trace-free
perturbation \emph{in the specified cylindrical frame}. The same
conclusion requires a verified change of frame when H8 rays are tilted;
pointwise orthogonal conjugation alone does not control the additional
derivatives of a spatially varying frame.

\section{A source-matched weighted dual obstruction}\label{sec:dual}

The coefficient condition \eqref{eq:eps} involves four trace-free
coordinates, not arbitrary physical tensor components. To test a
particularly strong candidate estimate, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualprofiles}
 P(r)=\begin{cases}(r-1)^2(2-r)^2,&1\le r\le2,\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
 \quad
 \eta(z)=\begin{cases}(1-z^2)^3,&|z|\le1,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Set $\rho(r,z)=P(r)\eta(z)\ge0$ and choose the meridional
source $f=P(r)\eta'(z)e_z$. This is source-matched in the literal sense
that $S=\rho\,e_z\otimes e_z$ is a compact symmetric inverse:
$\diver S=f$. Also $\int r f_z\dd r\dd z=0$, so the force
compatibility condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse} holds.

\begin{proposition}[A pressure-independent stress bound]
\label{prop:dual}
Let $\beta=15971/1720600$. No smooth compact symmetric tensor $H$
and smooth scalar pressure $q$ satisfy both
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualprob}
\begin{aligned}
 \diver H+\nabla q&=f,\\
 |H_{ij}|&\le\beta\rho\quad(i,j\in\{r,\theta,z\}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where components refer to the \emph{orthonormal cylindrical frame}.
The contradiction also persists for suitable smooth compact
nonnegative approximations to the profiles in \eqref{eq:dualprofiles}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Construct an axisymmetric vector field with components
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualtest}
 v_r=-rP\eta'',\qquad v_\theta=0,\qquad
 v_z=(2P+rP')\eta'.
\end{equation}
It is divergence-free because
$r^{-1}\partial_r(rv_r)+\partial_zv_z=0$.
The zero-extended profiles give a compactly supported Lipschitz $v$:
$P=P'=0$ at $r=1,2$, and $\eta'=\eta''=0$ at $z=\pm1$.
Thus integration by parts against $v$ is legitimate, by approximation
if necessary, and the pressure term vanishes. With the angular
factor $2\pi$ suppressed, the source pairing is
\begin{align}
 L&=\int_1^2\!\int_{-1}^1 r f_zv_z\dd z\dd r\notag\\
  &=\left(\int_1^2 rP^2\dd r\right)
    \left(\int_{-1}^1(\eta')^2\dd z\right)
    =\frac{1}{420}\,\frac{1024}{385}
    =\frac{256}{40425}.\label{eq:dualL}
\end{align}
Here the middle equality follows by integrating $r^2PP'$ by parts.
The physical cylindrical gradient of $v$ includes its hoop entry
$v_r/r$. Its entrywise absolute sum is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualgrad}
 |\nabla v|_1\le
 (4P+2r|P'|)|\eta''|+rP|\eta'''|
 +(3|P'|+r|P''|)|\eta'|.
\end{equation}
The elementary exact radial integrals are
$\int_1^2P=1/30$, $\int_1^2P^2=1/630$,
$\int_1^2rP^2=1/420$, and
$\int_1^2P|P'|=1/256$. On their supports,
$r\le2$, $|P''|\le2$, $|\eta''|\le6$ and
$|\eta'''|\le72|z|+120|z|^3$. Direct integration gives
\[
 \int_{-1}^1\eta=\frac{32}{35},\quad
 \int_{-1}^1\eta|\eta'|=1,\quad
 \int_{-1}^1\eta|z|=\frac14,\quad
 \int_{-1}^1\eta|z|^3=\frac1{20}.
\]
Consequently, writing $U=\int_1^2\int_{-1}^1
r\rho|\nabla v|_1\dd z\dd r$, one obtains the rigorous upper bound
\begin{align}
 U&\le\left(\frac4{420}+\frac2{64}\right)\frac{192}{35}
   +\frac2{315}\,24+\frac3{128}+\frac4{15}
   =\frac{313361}{470400}.\label{eq:dualU}
\end{align}
But \eqref{eq:dualprob} would imply $L\le\beta U$ by the virtual-work
identity $\int f\cdot v=-\int H:\nabla v$, whereas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualgap}
 L-\beta\,\frac{313361}{470400}
 =\frac{443015653}{2967690880000}>0,
\end{equation}
a contradiction. Nonnegative convolution approximations of $P$ and
$\eta$, supported away from the axis, converge in the requisite
derivative integral norms. The strict gap survives for sufficiently
small smoothing parameters and proves the smooth-profile assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Failure of the sufficient four-field envelope]
\label{cor:four-field}
For the source $f$ and envelope $\rho$ in
\eqref{eq:dualprofiles}, there is no fixed-frame four-coordinate
stress $H(A,B,C,D)$ of \eqref{eq:four-field-map} satisfying
$\diver H+\nabla q=f$ and the sufficient bound
\eqref{eq:eps}, even if the pressure is freely chosen.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:frame} transfers \eqref{eq:eps} to the
entrywise physical cap \eqref{eq:dualprob}. This contradicts
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}.
\end{proof}

The preceding argument excludes the \emph{stronger sufficient}
four-field estimate. It cannot alone exclude the original absolute
coefficient budget $\max_i|\delta c_i|\le(15971/122900)\rho$:
if one simply inserts that larger constant into the conservative
gradient bound \eqref{eq:dualU}, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weaker-inconclusive}
 \frac{15971}{122900}\frac{313361}{470400}-L
 =\frac{51024386641}{635933760000}>0.
\end{equation}
The sign of this bound is inconclusive, not evidence of existence.
The exact cone, however, has a smaller sharp dual support than
that componentwise comparison.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp dual of the original absolute coefficient cone]
\label{prop:sharp-cone}
For $\tau\ge0$ let $\mathcal K_\tau$ be the four-coordinate set
defined by \eqref{eq:exact-cone}. For arbitrary $a,b,c,d\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-support}
 \sup_{(A,B,C,D)\in\mathcal K_\tau}
 (aA+bB+cC+dD)
 =\tau\left[
 \max\!\left(\frac{|a|+|c|}{2},\frac{|d|}{4}\right)
 +\frac{|4a+b|}{8}\right].
\end{equation}
The upper bound in \eqref{eq:sharp-support} is proved in Lean for
the actual seven coefficient constraints.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $x=A-4B$. By \eqref{eq:exact-cone}, for fixed
$s=|D|\in[0,\tau/4]$, the variables $x,C,B$ are independent and
satisfy
\[
 |x|,|C|\le\frac{\tau-4s}{2},\qquad |B|\le\frac\tau8.
\]
The objective becomes $ax+(4a+b)B+cC+dD$. Its maximum for this
$s$ is
\[
 \frac{\tau-4s}{2}(|a|+|c|)
 +\frac\tau8|4a+b|+s|d|.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Source-shaped meridional flux, including viscosity]

$\inf_I|\kappa_2|>0$. If $b\in C^2(I)$ satisfies
$b(r_0)=b'(r_0)=0$ and another stress-shear contribution $R$
obeys, for finite constants $L,L'\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-edge-hypotheses}
 |R|\le L(|b|+|b'|+b^2),\qquad
 |\mathcal C+\mathcal V_\nu+R|\le L'b^2
 \quad\hbox{on }I,
\end{equation}
then $b\equiv0$ throughout $I$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating $M'b+Mb'$ divided by $r$ in
\eqref{eq:shaper-viscous} yields
$M b''/r+(2M'/r-M/r^2)b'+(M''/r-M'/r^2)b$,
which proves \eqref{eq:flat-edge-jets}. Let
$\eta=\inf_I|\kappa_2|>0$ and $L_b=\sup_I|b|<\infty$.
The two bounds in \eqref{eq:flat-edge-hypotheses}, boundedness of
the other three coefficients and $b^2\le L_b|b|$ yield a
constant $C<\infty$ such that
\[
 |b''(r)|\le C\bigl(|b(r)|+|b'(r)|\bigr)
 \quad(r\in I).
\]
By the initial conditions and the fundamental theorem of calculus,
the continuous nonnegative function $w=|b|+|b'|$ satisfies
$w(r)\le(1+C)\int_{r_0}^r w(s)\dd s$. Gronwall's elementary
integral inequality gives $w\equiv0$, and therefore $b\equiv0$.
\end{proof}

For the two profiles in \eqref{eq:shaper-rational-example}, direct
integration gives $m_1=16/15$ and $m_2=0$; hence $M=16/15$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flat-edge-explicit}
 \mathcal C+\mathcal V_1
 =-\frac{16}{15r}b''
 +\left(-\frac{32}{15}+\frac{16}{15r^2}\right)b'
 +\frac{64}{15r}b+\frac{256}{315r^2}b^2.
\end{equation}
At $r=2$ and $b=(r-1)^3$ this again gives
$-2036/315$. Since $\kappa_2=-16/(15r)$ is nowhere zero on
any annulus with $r>0$ (indeed $|\kappa_2|\ge8/15$ on
$1\le r\le2$), Proposition~\ref{prop:flat-edge}
prohibits a nontrivial one-sided flat cutoff from satisfying an
$O(b^2)$ budget for this \emph{restricted} flux whenever its remaining
terms obey the first inequality of \eqref{eq:flat-edge-hypotheses}.

In the H8 application, the two slice support functions in
\eqref{eq:complete-flux-supports} yield such a target bound only if
the relevant reference-centre-subtracted shear is precisely the
quantity in \eqref{eq:flat-edge-hypotheses} and both supports are
$O(b^2)$ on the edge collar. Neither follows from the source
architecture alone. Nontrivial compactness can instead require a
vanishing effective $b''$ coefficient, a compensating transition or
overlap term of second-jet size, a pressure boundary primitive with
the requisite source matching, or a density budget larger than
$O(b^2)$. The lemma does not rule out these possibilities and is not
a contradiction for the authentic H8 residual.

The Case~006 source specifies a compact curl localization
$u_b=\nabla\times(\chi A_c)$, separable poloidal and toroidal
shell shapers, and a toroidal pressure radial derivative, but does
not fix the smooth profiles, the complete four-patch interactions,
or a common pressure representative. Hence no value of
\eqref{eq:complete-flux} has yet been certified for the complete H8
residual. The exact code records the transition, shaper, viscosity,
overlap, reference-centre and axial boundary channels separately;
omitting any named channel blocks evaluation. Supplying zero for a
channel requires mathematical source justification, not merely
successful execution of the code.

\begin{proposition}[Quarter-turn localization and its nonlinear overlaps]
\label{prop:c4-overlap}
Let $Q(x,y,z)=(-y,x,z)$ and $\chi$ be invariant under $Q$. On a
simply connected Cartesian chart let $A$ be a smooth vector potential,
and denote the active push-forward by
$Q_*v(x)=Qv(Q^{-1}x)$. Write
\[
 v_j=Q_*^j\bigl(\nabla\times(\chi A)\bigr),\quad 0\le j\le3,
 \qquad u_b=\frac14\sum_{j=0}^3v_j.
\]
Then $u_b$ is solenoidal and equals the curl of the normalized
quarter-turn average of $\chi A$. Its full kinetic stress splits
exactly into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c4-stress}
 u_b\otimes u_b=\frac1{16}\sum_{j=0}^3v_j\otimes v_j
 +\frac1{16}\sum_{\substack{0\le j,k\le3\\j\ne k}}
     v_j\otimes v_k.
\end{equation}
For the material derivative, the same diagonal and ordered-cross
decomposition holds with $v_j\otimes v_k$ replaced by
$(v_j\cdot\nabla)v_k$. The twelve cross products do not generally
vanish under fourfold symmetry.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Rigid rotations commute with curl, and the curl of an average is
the average of the curls. Each $v_j$ is divergence free. Expanding
the product of the two sums for $u_b$ proves
\eqref{eq:c4-stress}; applying the Leibniz bilinearity of directional
differentiation proves the derivative formula. The normalization
$1/16$ follows from two factors of $1/4$ and cannot be dropped.
\end{proof}

For example, on a local chart take $\chi=1$ and
$A=(-yz,xz,0)$. Every rotated field is
$v_j=(-x,-y,2z)$, so in cylindrical notation
$u_r=-r$, $u_z=2z$, and $u_r u_z=-2rz$. The diagonal terms in
\eqref{eq:c4-stress} contribute $-rz/2$ to this shear, whereas
the ordered overlaps contribute $-3rz/2$. Thus three quarters of
the core shear is carried by the overlap partition even for an
exactly fourfold-equivariant field. This local polynomial example
does not claim a compact source profile; spatial disjointness, if
asserted for different carrier supports, must be checked separately.

\begin{proposition}[Shared-envelope second jet and core circulation]
\label{prop:shared-envelope}
Let $r_0<r<r_1$ be a cylindrical annulus separated from the axis,
with angular coordinate $\theta$ and fixed axial endpoints $z_-<z_+$.
Let $\mathcal A=(\mathcal A_r,\mathcal A_\theta,\mathcal A_z)$ be a smooth,
$2\pi$-periodic vector potential in the cylindrical orthonormal frame
(possibly only $C_4$-equivariant), and let
$U_c=\nabla\times\mathcal A$. Suppose the localized core and the
poloidal stream functions of Proposition~\ref{prop:shaper-shear}
share one radial envelope $b(r)$, so their total meridional
correction is obtained from
\[
 \nabla\times\left[b(r)
 \left(\mathcal A+
    \sum_i\frac{K_ia_i(r)\Xi_i(z)}{r}e_\theta\right)\right].
\]
An arbitrary smooth background independent of $b$ may be included.
Denote the full angular mean by $\langle\cdot\rangle_\theta$, set
\[
 M(r)=\sum_iK_i m_i a_i(r),\qquad
 V(r)=\int_{z_-}^{z_+}\langle (U_c)_z(r,\theta,z)
                  \rangle_\theta\dd z ,
\]
and let $S_b(r)$ be the axial/angular integral of the increment in the
physical shear
$K_{rz}=u_ru_z-\nu(\partial_zu_r+\partial_ru_z)$.
The coefficient of $b''(r)$ in $S_b$ is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-second-jet}
 \kappa_{2,\mathrm{eff}}(r)=
 -\nu\left[\int_{z_-}^{z_+}
      \langle\mathcal A_\theta(r,\theta,z)\rangle_\theta\dd z
         +\frac{M(r)}r\right].
\end{equation}
All the remaining terms in $S_b$ are expressions in $b,b'$
with smooth bounded coefficients on compact subannuli. In particular,
the quadratic overlaps among the four velocities contain no
$b''$ term in this \emph{physical shear} channel.

For any reference radius $r_*\in[r_0,r_1)$, put
\[
 H_*:=r_*\int_{z_-}^{z_+}
     \langle\mathcal A_\theta(r_*,\theta,z)\rangle_\theta\dd z.
\]
Then \eqref{eq:full-second-jet} has the potential-free expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circulation-second-jet}
 \kappa_{2,\mathrm{eff}}(r)=
 -\frac{\nu}{r}
 \left[H_*+\int_{r_*}^r sV(s)\dd s+M(r)\right].
\end{equation}
Consequently, \emph{identical} second-jet cancellation on an interval
requires and is implied by a single anchor equality
$H_*+\int_{r_*}^{r_a}sV(s)\dd s+M(r_a)=0$
and the differential identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circulation-cancellation}
 rV(r)+M'(r)=0
\end{equation}
throughout that interval. If the potential extends smoothly across
the axis on complete disks, one may set $r_*=0$ and $H_*=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing $A_\theta^{\rm tot}=\mathcal A_\theta+
\sum_iK_i a_i\Xi_i/r$, the cylindrical curl gives
\[
 u_r-U_r=b\left(\frac1r\partial_\theta\mathcal A_z
           -\partial_zA_\theta^{\rm tot}\right),\qquad
 u_z-U_z=b\left(\frac1r\partial_r(rA_\theta^{\rm tot})
              -\frac1r\partial_\theta\mathcal A_r\right)
             +b'A_\theta^{\rm tot}.
\]
The quadratic product $u_ru_z$ therefore uses at most $b$ and
$b'$. In the viscous shear only $\partial_r(u_z-U_z)$
produces $b''$, with coefficient $-\nu A_\theta^{\rm tot}$.
Angular/axial integration proves \eqref{eq:full-second-jet}.
Meanwhile
\[
 (U_c)_z=\frac1r\partial_r(r\mathcal A_\theta)
              -\frac1r\partial_\theta\mathcal A_r .
\]
The full angular average annihilates the last derivative; hence
$\partial_r(r\int\langle\mathcal A_\theta\rangle_\theta\dd z)
=rV(r)$. Integrating from $r_*$ proves
\eqref{eq:circulation-second-jet}. Differentiating its bracket
proves the stated characterization of identical cancellation.
Smooth axis extension forces $r\langle\mathcal A_\theta\rangle_\theta
\to0$ as $r\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

The annular reference circulation matters. For the single profile
$(K,a,\Xi)=(1,1,(1-z^2)^2)$ on $[-1,1]$, one has $M=16/15$.
Choosing the local, $C_4$-invariant azimuthal potential
$\mathcal A_\theta=-8/(15r)$ gives
$\int_{-1}^1\mathcal A_\theta\dd z=-16/(15r)$ and
$\kappa_{2,\mathrm{eff}}\equiv0$. Here $(U_c)_z=0$ on the
annulus, while $H_*=-16/15$ supplies the cancellation.
This is an algebraic counterexample to deleting the circulation
constant, not an admissible axis-spanning H8 core. If a smooth
axis-spanning potential is prescribed, its core axial flux rather
than a free annular circulation must supply the needed moment.

The proposition concerns the canonical physical $rz$ stress.
The pressure-compensated source relation
\eqref{eq:complete-flux} retains its independent axial jump; neither
pressure nor an H8 inverse stress is reconstructed here. If the
actual transition cutoff $\chi$ differs from the shaper envelope
$b$, the core contributions cannot be assigned to the same
$b''$ coefficient without an additional source identity. Thus
the genuine H8 test still requires the core flux $V$, the
reference circulation, the shared-cutoff decision, and the full
source-matched cone density and frame.

\begin{proposition}[Independent core and shaper cutoffs]
\label{prop:split-cutoff}
On an annular cylindrical patch let $\mathcal A$ be any smooth,
$2\pi$-periodic vector potential, let
$\chi=\chi(r,\theta,z)$ be a smooth core cutoff, and let $b=b(r)$
be an \emph{independent} radial poloidal cutoff. Put
\[
 P_\theta(r,z):=\sum_i\frac{K_i a_i(r)\Xi_i(z)}r,\qquad
 u=U+\nabla\times\left(
          \chi\mathcal A+bP_\theta e_\theta\right),
\]
where $U$ is a fixed smooth background. If $S(r)$ is the
axial/angular integral of the physical shear
$K_{rz}=u_ru_z-\nu(\partial_z u_r+\partial_r u_z)$, its full
cutoff-\emph{second}-derivative part is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split-second-jet}
\begin{aligned}
 S_{\mathrm{jet},2}(r)=-\nu\int_{z_-}^{z_+}
 \bigg\langle&
 \mathcal A_\theta(\chi_{rr}-\chi_{zz})
 +\frac{\mathcal A_z}{r}\chi_{\theta z}
 -\frac{\mathcal A_r}{r}\chi_{r\theta}
 +P_\theta b''\bigg\rangle_\theta\dd z .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The difference $S-S_{\mathrm{jet},2}$ contains the cutoffs and
their first derivatives, but no second derivative of either
cutoff. The nonlinear cross terms, including all ordered $C_4$
core overlaps, belong to this remainder. In particular, a
radial shaper $b$ and a genuinely three-variable $\chi$ do not
have one common second-derivative coefficient.

For any specified pressure representative $p$, the complete
annular \emph{spatial-divergence} source still obeys
\[
 q_{\rm s}(r)=
 [\langle u_z^2-2\nu\partial_z u_z+p
      \rangle_\theta]_{z_-}^{z_+},\qquad
 J_f=S-\frac{r_a}{r}S(r_a)
           +\frac1r\int_{r_a}^r s q_{\rm s}(s)\dd s.
\]
For the full unforced Navier--Stokes residual
$\mathcal N(u,p)=\partial_tu+\operatorname{div}K+\nabla p$,
one must also keep
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split-temporal-flux}
 T_z(r)=\int_{z_-}^{z_+}
         \langle\partial_tu_z\rangle_\theta\dd z,
 \qquad
 J_{\mathcal N}(r)=J_f(r)
       +\frac1r\int_{r_a}^r sT_z(s)\dd s .
\end{equation}
No smallness of the pressure primitive or the first-derivative
remainder follows from \eqref{eq:split-second-jet}; neither
$T_z$ nor its primitive may be set to zero without a source
identity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cylindrical curl identities give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 u_r-U_r&=\frac1r\partial_\theta(\chi\mathcal A_z)
              -\partial_z(\chi\mathcal A_\theta+bP_\theta),\\
 u_z-U_z&=\frac1r\partial_r
                \bigl(r(\chi\mathcal A_\theta+bP_\theta)\bigr)
              -\frac1r\partial_\theta(\chi\mathcal A_r).
\end{aligned}
\]
Each velocity contains at most one derivative of either cutoff;
therefore $u_ru_z$ contains no cutoff second derivative.
Differentiating the first line in $z$ and the second in $r$
produces exactly the four terms in \eqref{eq:split-second-jet}.
The rest contains only lower cutoff jets. The pressure identity
is the integrated axial component of
$\operatorname{div}K+\nabla p=f$ and does not identify an
independent radial primitive of $p$ with a time-only gauge.
Adding the axial marginal of $\partial_tu$ and integrating the
radial first-order equation proves \eqref{eq:split-temporal-flux}.
\end{proof}

The source distinction is substantive: the available Case~006
structured-stress manuscript prescribes the core cutoff as a
$C_4$-invariant function of similarity coordinates, equal to one
on an inner subcore, whereas its poloidal shaper uses a separately
specified shell profile $\Phi(r/\ell)\Xi(z/c)$. Those definitions
neither identify the two radial envelopes nor provide a complete
frozen four-patch source. Equation~\eqref{eq:split-second-jet}
is therefore the appropriate general decomposition; the
shared-envelope Proposition~\ref{prop:shared-envelope} is its
additional-hypothesis specialization.

\begin{proposition}[Certified variable-frame dual enclosure]
\label{prop:frame-enclosure}
Consider the one-sided seven-ray coefficient cone and its
trace-free representative $H$ in \eqref{eq:four-field-map}.
For every nonnegative density $\rho$, the pointwise coefficient
floors imply
\[
 |A|,|B|,|C|,|D|\le\frac{\rho}{4},
           \qquad \norm{H}_{F}\le\rho.
\]
Let a cylindrical shear-test slice be partitioned into finitely
many measurable cells $\Omega_j$. On each cell suppose the
physical orthogonal frame $E(x)$ admits a fixed rational
orthogonal frame $E_j$ and a \emph{certified} bound
\[
 \sup_{\Omega_j}\norm{E(x)-E_j}_{F}\le\delta_j,
 \qquad m_j=\int_{\Omega_j}\rho(x)\dd\mu(x).
\]
Here $\dd\mu=\dd z\,\dd\theta/(2\pi)$ on the slice. If
$\sigma^+(\lambda)$ is the exact unit-density one-sided support
and $G=e_r\otimes e_z$,
define
\[
 \widehat B_\pm=
 \sum_j m_j\,\sigma^+\!
   \left(\pm\lambda(E_j^{\mathsf T}GE_j)\right),
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_E=2\sum_jm_j\delta_j.
\]
Then the genuine spatially varying shear supports satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variable-frame-enclosure}
 \max(0,\widehat B_\pm-\varepsilon_E)
       \le B_\pm
       \le \widehat B_\pm+\varepsilon_E.
\end{equation}
These bounds remain valid for smooth nonanalytic frames and
densities, provided the cell masses and frame-error bounds are
rigorously enclosed. Sampling a frame at cell centres alone
does not provide such a certificate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Applying the exact support formula of
Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-support} to the eight signed coordinate
covectors gives the largest respective normalized absolute
extrema
\[
 \frac{51113}{245800},\quad
 \frac{15971}{983200},\quad
 \frac{19171}{122900},\quad
 \frac{19171}{491600}.
\]
Each is strictly less than $1/4$, so
$\norm{H}_F^2=2(A^2+B^2+C^2+D^2)\le\rho^2/2$.
For orthogonal $E,E_j$,
\[
 EHE^{\mathsf T}-E_jHE_j^{\mathsf T}
 =(E-E_j)HE^{\mathsf T}
   +E_jH(E-E_j)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The Frobenius pairing with $G$ consequently changes by at
most $2\rho\norm{E-E_j}_F$. Taking the pointwise supremum
over the coefficient cone, then integrating over each cell,
proves \eqref{eq:variable-frame-enclosure}.
\end{proof}

The dual bound controls values of the rotating stress, not its
divergence. In a Cartesian chart, $K=EHE^{\mathsf T}$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rotating-frame-connection}
 \partial_jK_{ij}
 =E_{ia}E_{jb}\partial_jH_{ab}
 +\bigl((\partial_jE_{ia})E_{jb}
       +E_{ia}(\partial_jE_{jb})\bigr)H_{ab},
\end{equation}
with repeated indices summed. Thus even constant $H$ can
generate a nonzero source when $E$ rotates. Cylindrical
components require their geometric connection terms as well.
An independently checked Cartesian rotation example and exact
two-cell rational support tests accompany these formulas.
Neither a bound on $\delta_j$ nor
\eqref{eq:variable-frame-enclosure} controls $\nabla E$,
so the weighted symmetric-divergence inverse and its
derivative budget remain open.

The form of a compact cutoff may be fixed in a \emph{test family}
without identifying it with the source's unfrozen choice. Define
\[
 h(s)=\begin{cases}
   0,&s\le0,\\
   \dfrac{\exp(-1/s)}{\exp(-1/s)+\exp(-1/(1-s))},&0<s<1,\\
   1,&s\ge1,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \chi_*(r)=h(r-1)h(4-r).
\]
Then $\chi_*$ is smooth and nonanalytic, zero outside $[1,4]$,
identically one on $[2,3]$, and equals $1/2$ at $r=3/2,7/2$.
As a radial function on the surrounding chart it is $C_4$-invariant;
the hole at $r\le1$ makes its extension across the axis smooth.
This provides a reproducible candidate for testing derivative
estimates, not a determination of the H8 similarity-coordinate cutoff.

The source's toroidal pressure prescription likewise fixes only
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c4-pressure}
 \partial_r p_T=\frac{2u_\theta T_\theta+T_\theta^2}{r}.
\end{equation}
For a chosen inner-radius normalization $p_T(a,z)=0$, its
representative is
\[
 p_T(r,z)=\int_a^r\frac{2u_\theta(s,z)T_\theta(s,z)
                         +T_\theta(s,z)^2}{s}\dd s.
\]
For $a=1$, $u_\theta=r$ and $T_\theta=rz(1-z)$ this gives
\[
 p_T=\frac{r^2-1}{2}\bigl(2z(1-z)+z^2(1-z)^2\bigr),
 \qquad [p_T]_{z=0}^{z=1}=0.
\]
By contrast $T_\theta=rz$ gives the nonzero pressure jump
$[p_T]_{-1}^{1}=2(r^2-1)$. The arbitrary radial integration trace
$C(z)$ must be fixed by the remaining physical pressure equations;
it is \emph{not} an arbitrary gauge of the complete Navier--Stokes
system.

To test all pieces in one local example, take $0\le z\le1$,
$U_r=-r$, $U_z=2z$, $U_\theta=r$, and the one-shaper data
$K=a=1$, $\Xi=z^2(1-z)^2$, $b=(r-1)^3$, $\nu=1$, with the
toroidal profile and pressure from the preceding zero-jump example.
The $C_4$ core shear is partitioned into a diagonal $-r/4$ and
ordered overlaps $-3r/4$; the shaper and viscous parts follow from
Proposition~\ref{prop:shaper-shear}. Directly integrating
$K_{rz}=u_ru_z-(\partial_zu_r+\partial_ru_z)$ gives exactly

 S(r)=\frac{-r^4-30r^3-2r+3}{30r^2},\qquad
 q_{\rm s}(r)=4,\qquad
 J_f(r)=\frac{-r^4+30r^3-32r+3}{30r^2}.
\end{equation}
At $r=3/2$ the exact values are $S=-63/40$ and $J_f=91/120$.
The independently computed axial source integral

The example uses a \emph{polynomial local core and shaper}, not

\section{The physical residual and terminal speed}

Define the viscosity-one residual
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 \mathcal N(u,p)=u_t+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\Delta u+\nabla p.
\end{equation}
For smooth $(u,p)$ and $(w,q)$, direct expansion yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:add}
 \mathcal N(u+w,p+q)=\mathcal N(u,p)+\mathcal N(w,q)
   +(u\cdot\nabla)w+(w\cdot\nabla)u.
\end{equation}
Both nonlinear cross terms are essential. Equation \eqref{eq:add},
with explicit differentiability assumptions in the upstream physical
definitions, is checked in Lean by the declaration
\texttt{UnforcedPatching.}\allowbreak
\texttt{navierStokesResidual\_add}.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional terminal retention]\label{prop:ret}
Suppose that $u$ has arbitrarily large speed at points with
$t\uparrow1$. If a correction $w$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subleading}
 \norm{w(t,x)}\le\alpha\norm{u(t,x)}+C_0,
 \qquad 0\le\alpha<1,\quad C_0\ge0,
\end{equation}
uniformly on $0<t<1$ and $x\in\T$, then $u+w$ also has
arbitrarily large speed arbitrarily close to time one.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The reverse triangle inequality and \eqref{eq:subleading} give
\[
 \norm{u+w}\ge\norm{u}-\norm{w}
 \ge(1-\alpha)\norm{u}-C_0.
\]
Given any target speed $M>0$ and any terminal neighbourhood,
choose a point therein where
$\norm{u}>(M+C_0)/(1-\alpha)$.
Then $\norm{u+w}>M$ at that point.
\end{proof}

This last implication is kernel-checked by the Lean theorem
\begin{center}
\small\texttt{UnforcedPatching.speedUnbounded\_add\_subleading}
\end{center}
It is deliberately conditional: no field $u$ meeting the singularity
premise, and no actual PDE correction $w$ meeting
\eqref{eq:subleading}, has been supplied by these lemmas.

\section{Remaining analytic requirements}

The compact meridional inverse of Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse} is a
properly restricted solvability result. It does not resolve the
weighted attachment problem because the desired source may have an
azimuthal component, a noncompact Leray-projected tail, nontrivial
pressure coupling, or incompatible moments. Even when compact
inversion is possible, the output may leave the required seven-ray
positive cone, especially as the support shrinks. The strict dual
obstruction in Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}, together with
Corollary~\ref{cor:four-field}, excludes the sufficient
four-coordinate estimate; Proposition~\ref{prop:original-obstruction}
also excludes the original absolute coefficient correction budget
for that same manufactured source. In contrast,
Proposition~\ref{prop:positive-diagnostic} shows that the tested
dual field has no obstruction for the larger one-sided cone; this
leaves other dual tests and all primal construction questions open.
The next mathematical question is whether the \emph{authentic}
source, perhaps using a tilted frame or a more flexible coefficient
allocation, admits a weighted inverse satisfying the one-sided
positive H8 ray conditions (or the absolute inequalities
\eqref{eq:exact-cone}) and all spatial
derivative bounds. The sharp cone description enables a direct
weighted dual optimization against the authentic source through
\eqref{eq:rotated-integrated}, without
replacing those inequalities by the factor-$14$ sufficient estimate.
No numerical conclusion can be drawn from this inequality until the
actual $g,\rho,R_*,E$ are supplied and the frame-derivative terms
in \eqref{eq:frame-derivatives} are controlled.

A complete unforced singularity construction must still provide a
single authenticated local profile, source-matched smooth shell
data, a scale-sharp weighted symmetric inverse respecting the
coefficient margin, a periodic nonlinear correction
with a proved right-inverse/contraction scheme, the full physical
zero-residual identity, and terminal retention for that actual
correction. Each claim requires a further source-to-Lean semantic
audit and a compiled theorem using the genuine PDE definitions.

Thus the present mathematical result is an exact compact stress
construction and several independently checked necessary or
conditional ingredients. The existence or nonexistence of a
finite-time singular solution to \eqref{eq:NS} with smooth nonzero
periodic initial data is \emph{not decided by this manuscript}.
